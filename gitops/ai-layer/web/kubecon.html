Setup: the two clusters, Datadog on stage

The 30-minute KubeCon NA 2026 breakout (Sessionize session 1249553). This page is the demo cut of the 2-hour workshop: two clusters, the room on their phones, and no hands-on lab setup for anyone in the room. Three challenges, C5, C6 and C7, each about seven minutes: explain the attack, three minutes of play on the public bot, the guardrail turned on and shown blocking it on the private bot, then the mechanism with its Datadog tab. Click a prompt to copy it; click a toggle to copy the private-terminal command. Drive from this page and read the timing from facilitation/kubecon-na-2026-run-of-show.md.

The two clusters. The public cluster (attackme.burritobot.co) has no guardrails at all and no Terminal button. The room attacks it from their phones in all three challenges. Kyverno is off there, so C6's deployment lands. The private cluster (burritobot-private.burritobot.co, which both presenters share) is the full IDP with every Kubernetes control armed and the AI guardrails present but toggled off at the start (run guards-off from its terminal the night before), so they can be turned on live. Terminal login on the private cluster is sprouts / sprouts. The toggles below go through the guard-proxy, which Argo CD does not revert and which keeps the cost counter alive. Never flip a guard with kubectl set env.
Datadog on stage. Two tabs: burritobot-public is the room, burritobot-private is the presenters, so the room's volume never buries the trace being shown. Every trace shown is pre-seeded the night before, one saved view per challenge in presentation order (C5, C6, C7), so nobody waits for a span. Datadog comes up when it explains the attack, not as a product tour. On the burritobot-public tab, e-mails, card numbers, SSNs and phone numbers in the room's prompts read [EMAIL], [CARD], [SSN] and [PHONE]: the public cluster's collector scrubs them before export (#647). Names and other free text are not scrubbed.

SEGMENT 2 · 7 min C5: the CEO's secret

The story

Michael and Whitney. The opener, chosen to land a bang: the bot prints a real-looking home address to chat. The story: the engineers gave the bot read access to every Secret in its namespace so it could reach its own model keys, and never thought another Secret would land there. ceo-personal-record did. SEGMENT 3 · 7 min C6: prompt injection through a support ticket

The story

Whitney and Michael. A frustrated customer asks the bot to read their support ticket, and names nothing else. BurritoBot fetches it with read_support_ticket from the fake ticket site (#640), so the hidden paragraph is never in front of the person who asked. It tells the bot to create maintenance-shell from the Joker villain image and to name the Deployment. On the public bot Kyverno is off, so it lands. The fix has two tiers on the private bot, shown in this order: admission control, armed from the start, refuses the Joker; then guard-input-on arms the input classifier, and the ticket never reaches the model.

Toggle · private terminalguard-mcp-on
Success marker, private botAfter guard-mcp-on: agentgateway's MCP authorization rule on evil-mcp's bind allows only the one registered tool, get_weather, so the gateway stops the rogue tools/call before the tool runs. The output guard from Segment 2 is still on, so a leaked token would come back redacted too. Say both halves, in that order. The claim on stage, after C7: tools are vetted through an MCP proxy and a registry allow-list, so the model can be fully persuaded and still find the capability gone. C6 stops the text before the model reads it; C7 lets the model be persuaded and removes the capability. Only the second still holds when the next model is more persuadable than this one. C3: read the recipe off the filesystem

Whitney sends · Michael names the controls: KubeArmor blocks in the kernel, and alerts

Success marker (block and detection, one command)The read returns Permission denied (prevention: KubeArmor block-recipe-snoop in the kernel), and KubeArmor's own alert for the denied attempt appears in the relay log ("PolicyName":"block-recipe-snoop", "Action":"Block").
