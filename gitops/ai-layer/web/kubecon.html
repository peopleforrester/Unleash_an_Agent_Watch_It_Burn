Setup: the two clusters, Datadog on stage

The 30-minute KubeCon NA 2026 breakout (Sessionize session 1249553). This page is the demo cut of the 2-hour workshop: two clusters, the room on their phones, and no hands-on lab setup for anyone in the room. Four challenges in the order shown. Click a prompt to copy it; click a toggle to copy the private-terminal command. Drive from this page and read the timing from facilitation/kubecon-na-2026-run-of-show.md.

The two clusters. The public cluster (attackme.burritobot.co) has no AI guardrails and no Terminal button. The room attacks it from their phones in every challenge. The private cluster (burritobot-private.burritobot.co, which both presenters share) is the full IDP with the AI guardrails present but toggled off at the start, so they can be turned on live. Both clusters start with the four Kubernetes guardrails (RBAC, Kyverno admission, KubeArmor and the egress NetworkPolicy) present and off (#774), so Challenges 1 and 2 land on either bot, until guardrails-on in Challenge 2 arms all four on both at once. The night before, run guards-off and guardrails-off from the private terminal. Terminal login on the private cluster is sprouts / sprouts. The AI-guard toggles below go through the guard-proxy, which Argo CD does not revert and which keeps the cost counter alive. Never flip a guard with kubectl set env.
Datadog on stage. Two tabs: burritobot-public is the room, burritobot-private is the presenters, so the room's volume never buries the trace being shown. Every trace shown is pre-seeded the night before, one saved view per challenge in presentation order (#648), so nobody waits for a span. Challenge 2 has no seed yet (#843): show the trace of Whitney's live attack in the burritobot-private tab, or the Grafana view. Datadog comes up right after each challenge's live attack, before its fix, to show what the attack did, not as a product tour. On the burritobot-public tab, e-mails, card numbers, SSNs and phone numbers in the room's prompts read [EMAIL], [CARD], [SSN] and [PHONE]: the public cluster's collector scrubs them before export (#647). Names and other free text are not scrubbed.

SEGMENT 4 · 7 min Challenge 3: prompt injection through a support ticket

The story

Whitney and Michael. Tee-up: BurritoBot also answers help-desk requests, backed by the support desk at support.burritobot.co, and it is told to honor what the desk says. Each visitor gets a generated witchy name on the chat page, which sends it with every question. Introduce ticket HC-666 yourselves: the desk no longer hands out an ask. It looks like a card complaint; under it, in white text on a white page, it tells the bot to deploy joker- plus the asker's name. Say the white-text reveal, do not demo it. The image is registry.burritobot.co's, the internal registry Kyverno trusts, so each person's Joker lands with Kyverno at Deny since Challenge 2's switch: Kyverno did its job, and the attacker used something you trust (the 2021 Kubeflow campaign ran its miner the same way). The presenters' attack on the private bot lands one too, then its trace. The fix has two AI-era layers on the private bot, shown in this order: least privilege, guard-agency-on, takes Deployments away from the bot (OWASP LLM06, Excessive Agency); then guard-input-on arms the input classifier, and the ticket never reaches the model.

SEGMENT 6 · 5 min The OWASP close, burritos only, then break it

OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, 2025 (genai.owasp.org, read 2026-10-29). Covered today: LLM01 Prompt Injection (Challenge 3, and Challenge 4 through a tool), LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure (Challenge 1), LLM03 Supply Chain (Challenge 4's unvetted MCP server), LLM06 Excessive Agency (Challenges 1 to 4), LLM10 Unbounded Consumption (the finale). Further reading: LLM04 Data and Model Poisoning, LLM05 Improper Output Handling, LLM07 System Prompt Leakage, LLM08 Vector and Embedding Weaknesses, LLM09 Misinformation. The mapping to challenges is ours, not OWASP's.
OWASP Kubernetes Top 10, 2025 (kubernetes-top10.owasp.org). With these in place most of today's attacks stop downstream: K02 Overly Permissive Authorization (Challenges 1 and 3) and K03 Secrets Management (Challenge 1), K04 Cluster Level Policy Enforcement (Challenge 2), K05 Network Segmentation (Challenge 2), K10 Logging and Monitoring (every trace shown).
ASI beat, the blast radius leaving the cluster (optional, 1 min). OWASP Agentic Security Initiative ASI03, Identity and Privilege Abuse: where a container compromise becomes a cloud-account one.
